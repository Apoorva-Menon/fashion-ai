# Body Type Skill: Hourglass

## Morphological Characteristics
- Bust and hip measurements roughly equal or well-balanced.
- Significantly narrower, well-defined waist (typically 8 to 10+ inches smaller than bust/hips).
- Gently rounded shoulders that align with hips.
- Weight gain tends to distribute evenly across hips, thighs, bust, and upper arms.

## What Suits Best (DOs)
### Fabrics
- Fluid, draping fabrics that hug curves without adding bulk: silk, matte jersey, crepe, light wool, modal, stretch cotton.
- Medium-weight fabrics that hold shape while accentuating the waist.

### Garment Types & Silhouettes
- **Tops**: Wrap tops, fitted V-necks, scoop necks, sweetheart necklines, peplum tops that cinch at the natural waist.
- **Bottoms**: High-waisted wide-leg trousers, tailored pencil skirts, bootcut or flared jeans that balance hip curves.
- **Dresses**: Fit-and-flare dresses, bodycon midi dresses, classic wrap dresses, belted shirt dresses.
- **Outerwear**: Belted trench coats, tailored single-breasted blazers that nip at the waist.

### Belt & Accessory Placement
- Belts at the natural waist (medium to wide belts) to highlight the waistline.

## What to Avoid (DON'Ts)
- Boxy, shapeless, or oversized garments (e.g. sack dresses, unbelted oversized tunics) that hide waist definition and make the frame look heavier.
- Heavy, stiff fabrics (thick tweed, rigid heavy denim, puffy quilting) that obscure contours.
- Straight, drop-waist silhouettes or empire waists that cut across the widest part of the bust without accentuating the waist.
- Ruffles or bulky detailing simultaneously placed at both shoulders and hips.

## Styling Narrative & Subtitle Direction
Emphasize balance and celebrate natural waist definition. Never add bulk that competes with natural symmetry.
